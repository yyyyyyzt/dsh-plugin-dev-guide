Abstract
1
Rule-governed document review requires explicit criteria, source-addressable evidence,
2
and a distinction between provisional ﬁndings and accountable human disposition. We
3
present HARK, an architecture and an executed system that represent review criteria as
4
rules with hard, hybrid, or soft operational authority. Deterministic veriﬁers produce
5
checks and candidate spans; a positional evidence gate checks submitted quotations; a
6
policy derives suggested verdicts; and decision records support feedback storage. Tech-
7
nical solution proposals are the sole empirical instantiation. On a ﬁxed developmental
8
regression corpus of 100 synthetic proposals with 253 planted defects, the combined con-
9
ﬁguration achieves micro-F1 0.798 versus 0.222 for a keyword baseline and 85% policy-
10
verdict agreement. Of 70 policy-non-Pass proposals, 15 are under-called and 10 receive
11
Pass, including four of 23 expected Fail proposals. Sixty-eight of 85 misses enter pending
12
review, without evidence of human resolution. Five development-team runs on three inter-
13
nal proposals demonstrate workﬂow and persistence, not independent review quality or
14
learning gains. The evaluated snapshot does not enforce authenticated human approval,
15
a complete effective-authority ceiling, or content-addressed conﬁguration recovery. These
16
remain target contracts rather than implemented guarantees. The contribution is an ex-
17
ecutable review architecture with bounded comparative results and explicit implementa-
18
tion boundaries.
19
Keywords: document review; review knowledge representation; operational authority;
20
evidence traceability; deterministic veriﬁcation; human disposition; feedback persistence

1. Introduction
22
Rule-governed, evidence-bearing document review combines three conditions: ex-
23
plicit review criteria, a source-addressable document, and a distinction between provi-
24
sional ﬁndings and accountable human disposition. Checklist-based inspection provides
25
a foundation for this task [1,2]. The computational problem is not only to generate com-
26
ments, but to represent which criteria a system can check, what evidence it can supply,
27
and which decisions remain with a reviewer. We study technical solution proposals for
28
a large utility’s online service platform: documents describing intended architecture, in-
29
terfaces, data ﬂows, implementation plans, and risks before implementation. Assurance
30
cases, compliance dossiers, and architecture designs are potential applications of the same
31
task formulation, not domains validated in this study. Explicit review criteria also arise
32
outside internal engineering practice: the EU Artiﬁcial Intelligence Act, for example, con-
33
ditions market access for high-risk systems on documented risk management, technical

The motivating prototype represented 24 expert rules through keyword lists and
37
prompts, without a persistent feedback layer. This arrangement made it difﬁcult to dis-
38
tinguish a requirement’s mention from its satisfaction or to connect a proposed ﬁnding
39

40
the evaluation. On the ﬁxed synthetic corpus, B0 recovers 18.6% of planted defects, pro-
41
duces 124 false ﬁndings against 47 true ﬁndings, and achieves policy-verdict agreement
42
of κ = −0.043. These results motivate a different representation and routing architecture;
43
they do not identify model failure or isolate the causal contribution of typing.
44
Consider the criterion that a proposal specify load testing before go-live. “No load
45
test is planned” and “Load testing plan: to be determined” mention the activity without
46
supplying an adequate plan. Conversely, “A pre-production capacity drill will measure re-
47
sponse time at 500 TPS before go-live” may provide relevant evidence without the phrase
48
“load test”. These illustrative formulations separate lexical presence, document location,
49
and substantive satisfaction. A deterministic cue can support a limited check or identify a
50
passage for review; it does not by itself establish that a testing plan is sufﬁcient.
51
We call the representation and routing task the review-knowledge ingestion problem:
52
how should review criteria, evidence expectations, and decision permissions enter an as-
53
sisted review system? Keyword checks offer reproducible surface tests; prompts convey
54
contextual instructions; retrieval supplies relevant prior material. None of these mecha-
55
nisms alone speciﬁes the complete contract linking a criterion, a document range, a pro-
56
posed ﬁnding, and its disposition. HARK combines them through explicit rule records
57
and review states rather than treating the model’s output as the ﬁnal decision.
58
Two design tensions guide the architecture. First, review criteria mix observable
59
structure with contextual judgement:
section presence can be checked mechanically,
60
whereas risk adequacy requires interpretation. Veriﬁability is therefore relevant to rout-
61
ing [4,5], but the ability to execute a lexical test must not be confused with complete de-
62
cidability of its business requirement. Second, useful decision history may inform later
63
reviews, yet recording a decision, authorising a knowledge change, and demonstrating
64
improved review quality are different accomplishments. A system must expose these
65
boundaries rather than infer human approval from a name or infer learning beneﬁts from
66
stored feedback.
67
We present HARK, which retains the prototype name without implying a veriﬁed
68
human-gating guarantee, and examine its design, its evaluated implementation snapshot,
69
and its validation boundaries. The system is realised as an extension of a general-purpose
70
agent host: a host-independent review engine carries the representation and decision logic,
71
and a thin host integration supplies storage, tools and delivery, which is what makes the
72
architecture reusable beyond a single deployment. The paper makes four contributions:
73
(1)
A task formulation for rule-governed, evidence-bearing document review and a rule
74
representation that separates evidence production, provisional ﬁndings, suggested
75
verdicts, and human disposition (Section 3.1).
76
(2)
An operational authority model with hard, hybrid, and soft routes, veriﬁer-
77
produced absence records, quotation checks, and a deterministic verdict policy,
78
together with the limits of those routes in the evaluated snapshot (Sections 3.2–3.5).
79
(3)
A host-independent engine with a thin deployment integration, carrying feedback
80
records, overrides, exemplars and review-state storage, and distinguished from the
81
target contracts for authenticated approval, complete effective-authority control,
82
and content-addressed recovery (Sections 3.6 and 3.8).

2. Related Work
104
Rule-governed, evidence-bearing document review.
105
Document inspection has a long history of using explicit review procedures and
106
checklists [1,2]. Requirements engineering adds quality criteria, smell detection, and trace-
107
ability to this tradition [4–6]. We use rule-governed, evidence-bearing document review to de-
108
note a task in which an organisation supplies review requirements, a system associates
109
proposed ﬁndings with evidence in the submitted material, and an accountable reviewer
110
resolves issues that the automated procedure cannot settle. Requirements speciﬁcations,
111
policy-governed contracts, and technical solution proposals provide different instances of
112
this task [7–9]. Security requirements and industrial certiﬁcation research further illustrate
113
the importance of explicit review criteria [10,11], while traceability has also been studied
114
for an in-the-wild augmented-reality learning service [12]. These sources motivate the

Requirements quality and operational veriﬁability.
118
Requirements-smell analysis detects cues such as weak wording, placeholders, and
119
missing units [6]. Veizaga et al. combine smell detection with recommendations based
120
on pre-trained language models [13], and AirReq applies smell detection and elimina-
121
tion to commercial aircraft system requirements [14]. HARK draws on these techniques
122
for structural checks and candidate localisation, but a surface cue is not by itself a com-
123
plete assessment of whether a requirement is satisﬁed. A further distinction concerns the
124
meaning of veriﬁability. As a requirements-quality attribute, veriﬁability concerns whether
125
satisfaction can be established through an appropriate veriﬁcation procedure [4,5]; it does
126
not mean that the current document parser can decide the requirement. HARK’s tiers are
127
operational assignments relative to the available parser, veriﬁer, representation, and rule
128
package. A requirement requiring measurements, external records, or an image may be
129
veriﬁable in principle while remaining outside this prototype’s deterministic checking ca-
130
pability. The tier therefore speciﬁes output eligibility within an implementation, not an
131
intrinsic quality ranking of the requirement.
132
LLM-assisted requirements review and evaluation.
133
Reviews of LLM applications in requirements engineering describe uses across extrac-
134
tion, analysis, and speciﬁcation [15]; a dual-perspective review distinguishes using LLMs
135
for code veriﬁcation from verifying their generated outputs [16]. MARV uses multiple role
136
agents and retrieval of standards for software requirements speciﬁcation validation [7].
137
ReqPlan-Eval explicitly couples evidence-aware routing with human requirements-to-
138
planning decisions and discloses the limits of its evaluation [8]. These approaches supply
139
relevant precedents for evidence use and reviewer involvement. The inspected MARV ma-
140
terial does not establish the detailed behaviour of document-range validation or persistent
141
feedback, so we do not infer that those capabilities are absent.
142
Hallucination research motivates checking generated claims [17], but its ﬁndings
143
should not be converted into a universal claim that models cannot perform determinis-
144
tic tasks. Long-context position effects [18], limits of intrinsic reasoning self-correction
145
without external feedback [19], and model-contingent polarity in a hotel-review anno-
146
tation task [20] concern distinct failure modes and settings. They motivate separating
147
model judgement from permission to issue a result, rather than proving the effectiveness
148
of HARK’s particular routing policy. Likewise, the evaluation literature [21] motivates
149
reporting the corpus, judge, and task conditions under which a mechanism was tested.
150
HARK’s deterministic-judge evaluation addresses this bounded mechanism question, not
151
the capabilities of arbitrary deployed LLMs.

Neuro-symbolic policy review.
153
Neuro-symbolic integration offers several ways for symbolic components to con-
154
strain learned components [22]. PolicyGuard compiles organisational policies into typed
155
relational rules, uses LLM extraction to ground atoms from evidence, and applies sym-
156
bolic rule evaluation [9]. Its v1 report also documents legal-expert reﬁnement during
157
review-engine construction and validation: Appendix G describes editing extraction ques-
158
tions, correcting rule-combination logic, and treating a triggered ﬁnding as a case-speciﬁc
159
exception while preserving the audit trail. It is therefore inaccurate to characterise Policy-
160
Guard as having no feedback or human maintenance. The comparison instead concerns
161
the role of the evidence-producing component and the lifecycle of review knowledge:
162
PolicyGuard reports atom grounding, symbolic evaluation, and local expert corrections;

Industrial review and editable feedback knowledge.
174
Code review provides adjacent evidence about learning from reviewer reactions, al-
175

176
Tantithamthavorn compare ﬁne-tuning and prompting for code review automation [24].
177
BitsAI-CR’s v1 architecture contains two ﬁne-tuned LLMs, RuleChecker and ReviewFilter;
178

182

183

184

185

186

187

188

189

190

191

192

193

194
Grounding, non-detection, and pending decisions.
195
Attributed generation links claims to cited sources [29]. Claim-level evaluation of
196
verbatim citation distinguishes quotation compliance from whether the quoted passage
197
actually supports the claim [30]. HARK adds a rule-associated, tool-side eligibility check:
198
the evaluated prototype locates a submitted quotation and checks overlap with veriﬁer-
199
provided candidate evidence for that rule. This located-overlap gate is narrower than a
200
guarantee of complete span containment or semantic entailment. A located quotation can
201
still be irrelevant or misinterpreted, and candidate retrieval can miss relevant material.
202
Non-detection also needs an explicit evidence boundary.
A veriﬁer-owned non-
203
detection record reports that a speciﬁed procedure did not ﬁnd expected material in its
204
declared checking scope; it is not proof that the requirement is unsatisﬁed in the world,
205
or that relevant content is absent from every representation of the document. HARK re-
206
serves such records for veriﬁer output rather than accepting a model-generated assertion
207
of absence. When a hybrid rule lacks usable candidates, or a proposed ﬁnding does not
208
meet its evidence conditions, an explicit pending state preserves the unresolved obligation
209
instead of turning it into either a pass or a conﬁrmed failure. Documents remain untrusted

Human control, memory, and version requirements.
213
Human-in-the-loop research distinguishes learning arrangements according to how
214
control is allocated between system and expert [32].
HARK adopts this control ques-
215
tion at the level of review rules and feedback events; the presence of a named re-
216
viewer or a stored approval text is not equivalent to authenticating the approving human.
217
Agent-memory research [33], self-reﬁnement [34], reﬂection-based memory [35], and self-
218
modifying agents [36] show multiple ways to reuse interactions or improve behaviour.
219
Neither prompting nor retrieval inherently precludes audit logs, persistent consolidation,
220
or versioned knowledge. Those properties depend on the surrounding storage, update,
221
and permission mechanisms.
222
Memory extraction can expose sensitive stored content [37]. HARK’s structured feed-
223
back records and short exemplars make stored ﬁelds explicit, but this representation has
224
not been shown to provide a privacy guarantee. More generally, bounded evolution re-
225
quires separately establishing the identity and authority of the approving actor, rejecting
226
updates that exceed an authorised permission ceiling, and retaining the actual content
227
needed to replay a prior knowledge state. The evaluated prototype provides operational
228
tier routing, a located-overlap gate, and stored feedback; complete human authentication,
229
permission-ceiling enforcement, and content replay are not yet implemented. Consequently, feed-
230
back storage and version labels are reported mechanisms, not evidence that every update
231
is human-authorised, reversible, or beneﬁcial over time.

3.1. Task Formulation and Scope
244
We study rule-governed, evidence-bearing document review. An instance consists of a
245
source-addressable document d, an explicit rule package K, a policy P for aggregating
246
ﬁndings, and an accountable reviewer. The system prepares rule-linked ﬁndings and un-
247
resolved items; the reviewer determines their disposition and the ﬁnal opinion. This task
248
is broader than technical proposal review but narrower than arbitrary document under-
249
standing: it requires explicit criteria, identiﬁable evidence sources and a meaningful dis-
250
tinction between a machine recommendation and an accountable decision. A source range
251
identiﬁes what was read, not whether its contents are true or sufﬁcient.
252
The empirical instance is preliminary review of Chinese technical solution proposals
253
before construction. Its primary business object is the adequacy of the proposed design, not
254
proof that the proposed implementation has already been deployed. A rule must specify
255
the lifecycle stage, applicability, expected evidence and predicate being checked. We re-
256
tain the released package and labels for the retrospective evaluation, rather than silently

3.7. Required Human-Approval and Recovery Contract
358
For deployment under a software-enforced human-approval claim, we specify a
359
stronger contract, not an already implemented extension. Effective authority is a tuple
360
of permitted decision subject, admissible evidence, no-candidate behaviour, severity, ap-
361
plicability, activation status and policy eligibility.
Every write path must validate the
362
merged conﬁguration against its approved ceiling, including parameter edits, draft acti-
363
vation, ﬁnding severity, enable/disable and reset.
364
A trusted host must issue an approval event after displaying the exact object and
365
action to an authenticated reviewer. The event must bind review/rule identity, input and
366
opinion/conﬁguration content, action and values, principal and consumption or expiry
367
state. An agent-provided name or quotation must not mint that event. Content changes,
368
cross-object use and replay invalidate approval. Final signing requires explicit disposition
369
of pending items or a separately authorised waiver with a reason; the interface must not
370
confuse “Pass on conﬁrmed ﬁndings” with review completion.
371
Recoverable versions require content-addressed immutable snapshots of rules, lexi-
372
con, overrides, policy and relevant parser/veriﬁer versions, linked to input and approval
373
records.
Restoration must recover the speciﬁed approved state and reproduce its ex-
374
pected behaviour on ﬁxed inputs while retaining the audit history. Acceptance tests must
375

376
provals; authority-changing parameter/draft/severity/reset paths; unresolved-item sign-
377

379

380

381

382

3.8. Implementation and Evaluated Version
385
HARK [39] is delivered as an extension package for a general-purpose conversational
386
agent host, which supplies model access, ﬁle conversion, structured questions, delivery,
387
JSON-backed storage and a web server. Hosting is a deliberate design position rather
388
than an implementation shortcut: a review system has to specify which party may retrieve
389
evidence, which may propose a ﬁnding, and which may commit a verdict, and anchoring
390
those permissions at a host boundary keeps them explicit and separable from review logic.
391
The engine itself is host-independent: the parser, veriﬁers, gate, policy, aggregation and
392
renderer are pure functions over rule packages, documents and recorded state, and the
393
engine modules import nothing from the host, so the review semantics can be executed
394
and audited without a particular deployment. The package deﬁnes seven storage objects,
395
nine tools, three state-query slash commands and a host-served dashboard while reusing
396
host authentication, ﬁle handling and delivery; it requires no separate application backend
397
or embedding index. That reuse is what keeps the integration surface small, but operation-
398
speciﬁc approval remains a separate requirement and is not supplied by a reviewer-name
399
ﬁeld.

532
described in Section 3.7. An anonymous trace tying inputs, gate outcomes, dispositions
533
and effective conﬁguration to each ﬁnal opinion would be needed to strengthen this work-
534
ﬂow evidence without publishing conﬁdential content.

5. Discussion
536
5.1. Answers to the Research Questions
537
RQ1: representation, operational authority, and implementation boundaries.
538
HARK makes review criteria inspectable as rule records with evidence expectations
539
and operational routes. The package contains 10 hard, 13 hybrid, and ﬁve soft rules; all
540
four blocker rules are hybrid. These tiers identify execution paths in this package, not in-
541
trinsic decidability of the corresponding business requirements. A successful lexical check
542
establishes its observable predicate, not the adequacy of a load-testing plan or security
543
design. The design separates veriﬁer-produced ﬁndings, gate-conﬁrmed ﬁndings, expert
544
adoption, and ﬁnal disposition. The evaluated snapshot implements parts of this separa-
545
tion but does not enforce the complete target authority contract: gate-conﬁrmed blockers
546
already affect the suggested verdict, and some hybrid structural failures are produced
547
directly by veriﬁers.
548
RQ2: ﬁxed-corpus detection and verdict trade-offs.
549
The combined S-full conﬁguration produces 168 true positives, no false positives, and
550
85 false negatives, compared with 47, 124, and 206 for B0. Micro-F1 increases from 0.222 to
551
0.798, a difference of 0.576; the mean paired per-proposal F1 difference is 0.564, a distinct
552
aggregation. This is a bounded improvement on a developmental synthetic regression
553
corpus. B0 and S-full differ in detectors, cue handling, candidate routing, and evidence
554
checks, and B0 does not use the ﬂag judge. The comparison therefore does not isolate
555
typing’s causal effect. Identical S-full and S−veriﬁer results show that this deterministic
556
judge does not expose an effect of wider span scoping, not that scoping is effective or
557
unnecessary for an LLM.
558
The verdict trade-off is one-sided but not benign. Agreement with policy-derived la-
559
bels is 85%. Of 70 policy-non-Pass documents, 15 are under-called and 10 receive Pass: six
560
expected Revise and four expected Fail. The latter four belong to a subset of 23 expected
561
Fail documents. The corpus contains 13 zero-defect documents and 87 with at least one de-
562
fect; the 30 policy-Pass documents are a different group, including 17 with minor defects.
563
No ﬁnding is raised on the 13 zero-defect documents. These results describe the speciﬁed
564
policy and labels, not reliable release decisions in operational use.
565
RQ3: traceability, gate acceptance, and unresolved workload.
566
The ﬂag judge submits 53 hybrid ﬁndings, of which 42 pass the gate and 11 fail the
567
quotation-length ﬂoor. Gate acceptance is 79.2%, not semantic evidence validity. The 43
568
hybrid true positives comprise those 42 accepted submissions plus one directly detected
569
structural failure. The current location predicate checks overlap with a candidate span;
570
neither overlap nor a genuine quotation establishes that the text supports the allegation.
571
Zero false positives in this conﬁguration are an empirical result, not a consequence of
572
positional grounding.
573
Of 85 missed defects, 68 appear in pending review and 17 remain unsurfaced. The
574
complete pending workload is 932 unique hybrid document–rule pairs, a mean of 9.32
575
per document and median of nine, excluding soft-rule items. Only 68 of these pairs match
576
planted defects. This label density is not pending-item precision: pending expresses un-
577
certainty, not an asserted defect. Referral coverage of 80% of misses provides no evidence
578
that a reviewer read, resolved, or corrected them. A suggested Pass based on eligible
579
ﬁndings is consequently not equivalent to a completed review.

RQ4: persistence, approval, and recovery.
581
The reported ﬁve development-team runs on three internal proposals, with two reach-
582
ing a ﬁnal verdict, provide workﬂow evidence. The reported 58 ﬁndings and subsequent
583
statistics and exemplars describe recorded activity, not independently audited quality or
584
learning gains. Decision records can be persisted and aggregated without establishing
585
that their origin is authenticated or that they improve later reviews. The evaluated ﬁnal-
586
verdict path accepts a reviewer string, and instruction quotations for selected edits are
587
length-checked rather than veriﬁed as human authorisation. Pending ﬁndings can remain
588
when a review is ﬁnalised.
589
The authority and recovery gaps extend beyond tier editing.
Changes to no-
590
candidate behaviour, draft activation, ﬁnding severity, and reset can alter effective deci-
591
sion behaviour without changing a stored tier. The displayed pack@ver+ovN summary
592
counts overridden rules rather than identifying their contents; different behaviours can
593
share the same label. Authenticated approval bound to the action and effective content,
594
control of all authority-changing operations, and immutable conﬁguration snapshots are
595
target contracts, not capabilities demonstrated by this snapshot. The observed counterex-
596
amples concern callable interfaces and do not establish malicious activity in the internal
597
runs.
598
5.2. Implications for Evidence-Bearing Document Review
599
The useful abstraction is a review record linking a criterion, an evidence object, an
600
operational route, and a disposition. This complements comment-generation approaches
601
and evidence-aware review workﬂows [8,24]: it makes the inputs to a recommendation
602
inspectable without treating inspectability as correctness. Human approval remains a
603
design requirement whose engineering implementation must be distinguished from or-
604
dinary operator-led workﬂow.
605
The same abstraction could support assurance cases, compliance dossiers, or architec-
606
ture designs if each supplies explicit criteria and addressable evidence. Such adaptation
607
would require domain-speciﬁc evidence objects, lifecycle assumptions, rule provenance,
608
and disposition responsibilities. This study validates none of those additional applica-
609
tions. Nor does narrow model input eliminate prompt injection or feedback-storage pri-
610
vacy risks [31,37]. The present evidence supports the architecture’s executable composi-
611
tion and its ﬁxed-corpus trade-offs, while exposing the limits that govern its interpreta-
612
tion.
613
6. Limitations and Future Work
614
Developmental synthetic evaluation.
615
The corpus reuses phrase pools and was developed alongside the rule package. Its
616
planted labels and expected verdicts reﬂect the generator and the same policy used for
617
evaluation; the self-check is an execution-consistency check that allows planted defects to
618
remain pending or missed. The comparison is therefore a regression result, not an indepen-
619
dent estimate of semantic review accuracy. Documents are the resampling unit; 253 defects
620
and 2300 veriﬁable rule–document pairs are not independent document samples, and byte-
621
identical deterministic repetitions add no sampling evidence. The reported statistics are
622
descriptive, and no conﬁdence intervals or signiﬁcance tests are claimed. Generalisation
623
would require frozen development artefacts and independently labelled, source-separated
624
material.
625

Judge and component attribution.
626
The ﬂag judge reacts to veriﬁer cues rather than interpreting design intent. Its recall
627
and precision are neither general lower nor upper bounds for an LLM, which could both
628
recover semantic defects and introduce unsupported ﬁndings. The end-to-end compar-
629
ison changes multiple components and does not identify typing’s individual effect. No
630
reported experiment establishes cross-model stability, semantic entailment at the gate, or
631
the beneﬁt of span scoping for generative judges. Controlled component tests and model-
632
based evaluations are possible extensions, not results of this study.
633
Human approval and effective authority.
634
The evaluated implementation assumes a trusted operator-led workﬂow; it does not
635
authenticate human approval through its reviewer-name or instruction-quotation ﬁelds.
636
Pending issues can survive ﬁnalisation. Existing tier restrictions do not cover the full
637
effective-authority surface, including parameter changes, draft activation, ﬁnding-level
638
severity, and reset. A stricter implementation would need approvals bound to a trusted
639
principal, action, object, effective content, and consumption state, together with explicit
640
disposition or waiver of unresolved items. These controls are planned and have not been
641
implemented or validated here. Their absence limits assurance claims but does not imply
642
that the internal runs involved misuse.
643
Version recovery and feedback use.
644
The override-count version label is a display summary, not a unique content identi-
645
ﬁer. It cannot alone reproduce a past review or distinguish different conﬁgurations with
646
the same number of overridden rules. The target recovery contract would preserve the ef-
647
fective rules, lexicon, overrides, policy, parser and veriﬁer versions, and an audit cursor in
648
an immutable snapshot. Current persistence and aggregation evidence does not establish
649
complete historical replay, authenticated attribution of every update, or net beneﬁts from
650
feedback. No longitudinal improvement claim is made.
651
Domain, lifecycle, and input coverage.
652
The 28-rule Chinese-language package concerns one organisation’s technical pro-
653
posal review. Hard denotes the snapshot’s automatic route and may use a lexical or struc-
654
tural proxy narrower than the business criterion. Requiring Mermaid in the current pack-
655
age reﬂects a parser-visible check; an unreadable image does not by itself prove a defective
656
design without a governing format requirement. Likewise, future-tense implementation
657
plans must be judged against proposal-stage criteria rather than treated as failed imple-
658
mentation acceptance. Changing these predicates, labels, tiers, or evidence rules would
659
require revalidation and, where affected, recomputation; the current metrics cannot be
660
reassigned to a revised task. Other languages and document families require their own
661
evidence mapping and validation.
662
Workﬂow evidence and human factors.
663
Three internal documents and ﬁve development-team runs, only two carried to a ﬁ-
664
nal verdict, are insufﬁcient to establish external reviewer acceptance, reduced workload,
665
or better decisions. The reported counts are not an independent audit of the private pi-
666
lot store. Pending referral is not human resolution, and agreement between suggested
667
and ﬁnal verdicts is not a direct measure of rubber-stamping. Human disposition quality,
668
time costs, missed pending items, and automation bias remain unmeasured. Evaluation
669
of these outcomes must also distinguish provisional recommendation from review com-
670
pletion.

7. Conclusions
672
HARK represents rule-governed, evidence-bearing document review through ex-
673
plicit criteria, operational authority routes, located evidence, provisional ﬁndings, and
674
decision records. Technical solution proposals are the sole empirical instantiation; assur-
675
ance cases, compliance dossiers, and architecture designs remain potential applications.
676
The system contribution is the executable combination of these review objects and inter-
677
faces, not a guarantee that a model, veriﬁer, or recorded operator name establishes an
678
accountable ﬁnal decision.
679
On the ﬁxed synthetic regression corpus, the combined conﬁguration achieves micro-
680
F1 0.798 versus 0.222 for B0, with 168 true positives, no false positives, and 85 misses.
681
Policy-verdict agreement is 85%; 15 of 70 policy-non-Pass documents are under-called, in-
682
cluding 10 suggested Pass and four Fail-to-Pass cases among 23 expected Fail documents.
683
Sixty-eight misses enter pending review, without demonstrated human resolution. The re-
684
ported development-team runs support workﬂow and feedback persistence but not inde-
685
pendent quality or online learning beneﬁts. Authenticated approval, a complete effective-
686
authority ceiling, and content-addressed recovery remain unimplemented target contracts.
687
These boundaries deﬁne what the present design and validation evidence supports.
