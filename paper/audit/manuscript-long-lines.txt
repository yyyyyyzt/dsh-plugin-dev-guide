\abstract{Large language models (LLMs) increasingly pre-screen technical documents before an expert signs off, yet
expert review knowledge enters such systems as keyword rules, prompt checklists or retrieval over past reviews, and each
route loses a property that high-stakes review needs: findings must trace to a rule and a document location, decision
authority must match how deterministically a requirement can be checked, knowledge must grow from use, and that growth
must stay under human control. We call this the \emph{review-knowledge ingestion problem} and study it for preliminary
review of technical solution proposals at a large utility. We present HARK (Human-Gated Authority-Typed Review
Knowledge), which answers the four properties with four mechanisms rather than one. Knowledge becomes a versioned
package of verifiability-typed rules: \emph{hard} rules are owned by deterministic verifiers that produce located
evidence, including absence evidence an LLM may never assert; \emph{hybrid} rules let verifiers locate candidate spans
while the LLM judges only inside them, enforced by a tool-side evidence gate; \emph{soft} rules only raise items for
judgement. A deterministic policy derives the suggested verdict; only the expert writes the final one. Expert decisions
become an audited learned layer that may tune parameters and phrasing but never promotes a rule's tier. Implemented as a
plugin of an open-source agent harness (28 rules, no separate backend), the design is evaluated with a deterministic
judge that decides only inside verifier-located spans; all numbers below are conditional on that judge, not on a
particular large model. It reaches F1 0.798 against 0.222 for a keyword baseline on 100 synthetic proposals with 253
planted defects, with no finding raised on a clean proposal and 85\% verdict agreement ($\kappa=0.76$); 80\% of the
remaining misses reach the expert as open items, at the cost of under-calling 15 of the 70 defective proposals, four of
them to a pass. Only 10 of 28 rules proved deterministically verifiable, and the four blocking rules least of all.}
